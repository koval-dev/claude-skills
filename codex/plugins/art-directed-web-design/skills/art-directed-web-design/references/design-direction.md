# Choosing a Design Direction

Choose the direction that fits the content, audience, assets, and existing design system. These are lenses, not fixed section templates.

## Swiss editorial

Useful when information, credibility, and structure matter most. Use strong column alignment, asymmetric text and media placement, restrained colour fields, sharp scale contrast, and controlled whitespace. Rules and compact captions can support hierarchy; apply the skill's numbering rule when considering numeric wayfinding.

## Institutional modernism

Useful for education, public service, cultural, and research content. Pair a broad content grid with narrower supporting columns, purposeful brand-colour blocks, factual captions, and predictable interactions. Give the page character through proportions and typography.

## Image-led editorial

Useful when strong photography or video exists. Integrate media into the grid, pair large imagery with compact text, and use crops and captions deliberately. Keep text readable and let quieter sections follow strong media moments.

## Typographic campaign

Useful for concise promotional or event pages. Use disciplined alignment and dramatic scale differences for headlines, real dates, and calls to action. Repeat a restrained motif and keep supporting copy readable.

## Selection test

Prefer the direction that fits the audience, can be expressed with the current design system, creates a recognizable silhouette, and retains its idea on mobile. Preserve a direction the user has already chosen.
